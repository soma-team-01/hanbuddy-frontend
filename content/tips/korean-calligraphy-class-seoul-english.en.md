## What happens in a session?

The activity page lists three steps:

1. **Line practice, 40 minutes.** An introduction to *munbangsau* (문방사우), the four treasures of the study (brush, ink, paper and inkstone, per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Treasures_of_the_Study)), how to hold a brush, and line drawing.
2. **Phrase practice, 50 minutes.** You choose what to write and practice it.
3. **Scroll, 20 minutes.** You write on a *jokja* (traditional hanging scroll), stamp your seal and sign. You can also finish extra postcards.

## Can I write my name in Korean Hangul?

Yes. Writing your name in Hangul is the main option on the class page, and you do not need to know Hangul beforehand. Hangul is an alphabet: its 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels are grouped into square syllable blocks of an initial consonant, a vowel and an optional final consonant ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul)). So a name is spelled by sound, one block per syllable. For example, 사랑 (*sarang*, love) is two blocks: 사 (ㅅ + ㅏ) and 랑 (ㄹ + ㅏ + ㅇ).

If you would rather write a word, the page suggests *sarang* (사랑, love), *haengbok* (행복, happiness) or *haeng-un* (행운, good fortune). If you want to check your name's Korean spelling first, message HanBuddy before the day.

## What do I take home?

A *jokja*, a traditional hanging scroll, with your writing, your seal and your signature, ready to hang on a wall. If you want more pieces, you can add postcards. Equipment rental is included.

## What is seoye, and why does it matter in Korea?

*Seoye* (서예) is the Korean name for calligraphy, the Korean tradition of artistic writing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_calligraphy)). In January 2025 the Korea Heritage Service designated writing Hangul with ink and brush, and the knowledge behind it, a National Intangible Cultural Heritage, as a community item without named holders ([The Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/lifestyle/people-events/20250123/korean-calligraphy-elevated-to-intangible-cultural-heritage-status)). Hangul itself was officially published in 1446 under King Sejong ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangul)).
