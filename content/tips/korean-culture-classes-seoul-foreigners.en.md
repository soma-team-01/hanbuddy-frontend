Yes, foreigners can take a traditional Korean culture class in Seoul in English, and no Korean or art experience is needed. On HanBuddy, artist Damdamhi teaches two such classes in Korean, English and Japanese in Jamsil: [Hangeul calligraphy](https://hanbuddy.kr/en/activities/8), where you write your name on a hanging scroll, and [minhwa folk painting](https://hanbuddy.kr/en/activities/7), where you paint a traditional design to take home. Calligraphy takes about 110 minutes and minhwa about 2 to 3 hours, and all materials are prepared. Class details are as listed on HanBuddy's activity pages on November 8, 2026.

## Calligraphy or minhwa: which class fits you?

Both classes are seated, hands-on and end with a finished piece. The difference is what you make and how long you spend on it.

| | Calligraphy (*seoye*) | Minhwa folk painting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you do | Practice brush lines, then write your name or a Korean phrase in Hangeul, the Korean alphabet | Learn the stories behind a traditional design, then trace, outline and layer colors with Korean brushes and paints |
| What you take home | A *jokja* (traditional hanging scroll) with your seal and signature, plus extra postcards if you want | A finished painting to hang on a wall or use as decor |
| Length | About 110 minutes (40 + 50 + 20) | About 2 or 3 hours, depending on the painting |
| Choice | Your name, or a phrase such as *sarang* (love), *haengbok* (happiness) or *haeng-un* (good fortune) | Peony, magpie and tiger, or the character 福 (*bok*, good fortune) |
| Suits | A shorter class and a personal souvenir with your name in Korean | A longer, relaxed session and a colorful piece of art |

If you are short on time or want a gift with a name on it, choose calligraphy. If you want to spend an afternoon painting and like the idea of symbols with hidden meanings, choose minhwa.

## What happens in the calligraphy class

The calligraphy activity page lists three steps:

1. **Line practice (40 min).** You learn about *munbangsau*, the "four treasures of the study", how to hold a brush, and draw lines until the brush feels familiar.
2. **Phrase practice (50 min).** You choose what to write, your name in Hangeul or a phrase with a positive meaning, and practice it.
3. **Hanging scroll (20 min).** You write the phrase on a *jokja*, stamp your seal and sign it. Anyone who wants more pieces can also finish additional postcards.

Damdamhi notes that the same letters look different depending on who writes them, so no two scrolls come out alike.

## What happens in the minhwa class

Minhwa is the folk painting Koreans once made to wish for happiness, health, love and good fortune. You choose one of three designs:

- **Morando (peonies):** a flower painting symbolizing wealth, honor and happiness.
- **Kkachi horangi (magpie and tiger):** a humorous tiger painting said to bring good news and luck.
- **Bok munjado:** a decorated painting of the Hanja character 福 (*bok*) for happiness and good fortune.

The class starts with the stories behind the animals, flowers and colors. You then learn to use a traditional brush and go through the full process: transferring the outline, drawing lines and layering colors. Prepared outlines mean beginners can follow along, and the teacher guides you step by step at your own pace. At the end, you look at the finished pieces together and take photos.

## Do you need to speak Korean or be good at art?

No. Both activity pages say the classes are for first-timers: it is fine if you do not know Hangeul at all, have never held a brush, or have no art experience. The teacher stays beside you through each step, and the class is hosted in Korean, English and Japanese.

## What to look for when choosing any culture class

Whichever class you consider, four questions decide whether it will work for you:

1. **Is the class itself taught in your language,** not just the booking page?
2. **What do you leave with,** and is it easy to carry home?
3. **Are materials included,** or do you need to bring or buy anything?
4. **How long does it take,** and does that fit the rest of your day?

Damdamhi's classes answer them this way: English instruction, a scroll or painting to take home, materials included, and about two to three hours.
